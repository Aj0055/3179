Queensland's libraries: where they are and who they reach

More than 300 libraries serve Queensland, from Brisbane's city branches to one-room libraries hundreds of kilometres from the coast. Do people use them equally everywhere?

Libraries follow the people, and also the long roads

Most libraries cluster along the south-east coast, but small branches reach deep into the outback. Many of these are run through Rural Libraries Queensland, a partnership for councils with fewer than 15,000 people. Indigenous Knowledge Centres serve Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander communities, often with the only library for hundreds of kilometres.

Source: State Library of Queensland, Queensland public libraries directory (2026). 311 branches and community libraries shown.

Busy doors, big and small

Visits tell a different story from loans. Brisbane and the Gold Coast draw millions of visits a year, but many libraries are places to use computers, meet people and get help, not only to borrow books.

Source: Queensland Public Libraries Statistical Bulletin 2024-25, State Library of Queensland.

Borrowing is far from even

In 2024-25 the average Queenslander borrowed 7.7 items. Gold Coast residents borrowed 12.3 each. Burke Shire borrowed 0.2, though very small councils can show extreme figures from tiny populations and incomplete counts. Sixteen Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander councils appear in grey because the bulletin publishes no statistics for them.

Source: Queensland Public Libraries Statistical Bulletin 2024-25, State Library of Queensland (Feb 2026). Loans include physical and digital items.
